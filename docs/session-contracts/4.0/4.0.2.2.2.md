## Session 4.0.2.2.2 — Access projection and revocation

**Sub-version:** 4.0.2.2
**Master plan:** `docs/VERSION_4_0_PLAN.md` §4.0.2.2
**UX gate:** No
**Execution profile:** Frontier autonomous coding agent
**Delivery unit:** One agent session, one shared sub-version branch, one sub-version PR
**Roadmap coverage:** §4.0.2.2 durable-to-collaborative access projection, teardown, resynchronization, revocation invalidation and subscription-cost proof
**Internal phases:** 1. Resolve the projected role claim and one-way writer; 2. Prove teardown, resynchronization and revocation invalidation; 3. Measure and record the access/read-set cost
**Split triggers:** The projection cannot remain regenerable and one-directional without changing the durable access authority shipped in 4.0.1.1

## 1. Objective

Complete the sole collaborative authorization path by projecting the minimum
durable access state into the shipped gate and proving that revocation removes
access immediately.

## 2. Current context and dependencies

- **DEP-1:** Session 4.0.2.2.1 shipped the chain schema, gate contract and gate-first fixture functions.
- **DEP-2:** Sub-version 4.0.1.1 owns durable map/access authority and principal resolution.
- **DEP-3:** Decisions D3 and D5 require a regenerable Neon-to-Convex projection with no reverse write and no second access path.

## 3. Done conditions

- **DC-1:** The access projection carries only the role claim the collaborative gate needs and is written from the durable side on grant changes.
- **DC-2:** Tearing down and resynchronizing the projection restores correct access for every principal.
- **DC-3:** Revocation invalidates a live authorized read rather than leaving stale map data readable.
- **DC-4:** The deterministic per-subscription access/read-set cost is measured and recorded in `docs/SCRATCHPAD.md`.

## 4. In scope

- **IS-1:** The one-way projection writer, projected role shape and gate consumption.
- **IS-2:** Teardown, resynchronization, revocation and cost evidence.

## 5. Out of scope

- **OOS-1:** The production canvas subscriptions and calm no-access UI, which belong to 4.0.2.3.
- **OOS-2:** Access editing, map creation and archive behavior, which belong to 4.0.4.4.

## 6. Hard constraints

- **HC-1:** The projection is regenerable, minimal and never authoritative.
- **HC-2:** No write flows from Convex to Neon, and no second access-check path exists.
- **HC-3:** Revocation participates in every protected read set through the shipped gate.

## 7. Decisions the session plan must resolve

- **PD-1:** The exact projected role claim and its mapping to durable roles.
- **PD-2:** The durable-side trigger and idempotent resynchronization boundary.
- **PD-3:** The deterministic method for measuring the subscription cost.

## 8. Acceptance criteria

- **AC-1:** Granting access writes the minimal projection and the gate returns the matching role — proving DC-1.
- **AC-2:** Projection teardown followed by resynchronization restores the complete expected principal set — proving DC-2.
- **AC-3:** Revoking access invalidates an already-live protected read — proving DC-3 and HC-3.
- **AC-4:** A reproducible measurement and its result are recorded in the scratchpad — proving DC-4.

## 9. Verification

- **V-1:** Projection grant, teardown and resynchronization coverage.
- **V-2:** Live revocation-invalidation evidence.
- **V-3:** Deterministic cost evidence and the repository's standing definition-of-done gate.
